## 1. Target and how we count

**Benchmark (Kumopack Workshop, 2026-09-29, aggregate figures from their public template listing):** 881 mockup templates. Roughly 85 % are paperboard folding cartons; by title keyword (overlapping, indicative only) they concentrate on tuck-end (~270), inserts (~160), displays (~130), trays (~110), flip-top (~75), snap-lock (~75) and auto-lock (~55). Coverage is thin or absent for corrugated shipping styles (~30 titled as cartons), rigid boxes (~65), paper bags (~8), and there are **no pouches, tubes, labels or shrink sleeves**. Their templates are visual mockups; they are not tied to a price or to a factory that can make them.

**Our rule for counting — only producible structures count:**
- A **base structure** is a construction with its own generator (e.g. tuck-end carton, roll-end mailer, stand-up pouch).
- A **structural variant** is a combination of structural options that changes the dieline or the piece set (closure pair, window, hang tab, insert, handle, joint type, tear strip, adhesive strip, partition grid, pouch features …). Material, flute, print and size do **not** create a variant.
- A variant is counted when it passes every quality gate in §4 and has a pricing path (instant or RFQ). Since D-407 a variant no partner can make yet still counts and is shown — as a quotation request — so customers can design it.

**Targets:**
| Milestone | Base structures | Structural variants | Categories covered |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wave 1 (launch) | 41 | ≈ 580 | folding, corrugated, rigid (RFQ), pouch, paper bag, label, tag & sleeve, inserts |
| Wave 2 (+ ~8 weeks) | 90 | ≈ 910 — **passes 881** | + displays, food service, ready-glued corrugated, bulk |

We beat the benchmark on three axes at once: **more variants, far wider category coverage, and every one of them is priced and makeable.**

## 3. Architecture that makes the numbers cheap
1. **Composable construction modules** instead of one generator per style: body modules (4-panel tube, tray, tapered pail, n-gon tube, pouch web), closure modules (straight/reverse/airplane tuck, seal, snap-lock 1-2-3, crash-lock, four-flap lock, hinged lid, petal, gable), feature modules (window, hang tab, thumb notch, tear strip, adhesive strip, hand holes, vents, dispenser, neck lock), and piece sets (lid + base, sleeve + drawer, body + insert). A style is a **recipe** of modules plus constraints.
2. **Constraint solver per recipe**: min/max for every dimension derived from the modules (e.g. tuck depth vs width, crash-lock minimum depth, window margin to creases), caliper compensation per material class, and incompatibility rules (e.g. seal top + four-flap bottom excluded).
3. **Automatic 3D**: every module declares its hinges, so the fold sequence and the 3D model are generated, not hand-modelled; open/close animation comes free.
4. **Automatic assets**: thumbnail (2D + 3D), dieline SVG/PDF/DXF, spec-sheet fragment and TH/EN description generated per variant from the recipe.
5. **Data, not code, for the catalogue**: recipes live in versioned JSON; adding a variant is a data change reviewed by prepress, not a code release.

## 4. Quality gates (every variant, in CI)
- Cut lines form closed loops (≤ 0.01 mm), no overlapping faces, no zero-width panels.
- **Virtual fold test**: the generated 3D model closes into a box whose outer bounding box matches the expected L × W × H ± 1 mm for three sizes (min, typical, max) and two calipers.
- Collision check for closures (tuck and dust flaps do not intersect when closed).
- Manufacturability rules per material class: minimum panel width, minimum distance between cut and crease, slot width vs caliper, glue-flap width, window-to-crease margin, sheet-size fit for at least one partner machine.
- Snapshot of dieline + thumbnail reviewed once by prepress; later changes that alter a snapshot need an approval.
- Mapped to ≥ 1 capable partner and to a pricing path (instant calculator or RFQ); otherwise the variant stays hidden.

## 5. Beyond the generator — how we keep growing
- **Partner dieline intake**: partners upload proven dielines (DXF/PDF) of structures they already make; prepress converts them to parametric recipes or registers them as fixed-size templates with the partner's permission recorded. Each becomes a new base or variant after the gates.
- **Structural design service**: when no style fits, the customer describes the product (dimensions, weight, use); a structural designer creates a new recipe (paid or free per policy) and it enters the library for everyone once approved.
- **Demand-led roadmap**: library searches with no result, RFQs for unlisted structures and target-price misses feed a monthly "next styles" list.
- **Scenes and mockups**: our own lighting/scene presets for 3D renders (product photography style), never third-party mockup assets.

## 6. What we never do
- Import, trace or re-model another platform's templates, mockups or scene files, or reuse their model IDs or category wording.
- Count mockup images, colourways or sizes as styles.
- ~~Publish a style customers can design but nobody can make.~~ Superseded by D-407 (2026-10-15): every drawn, checked and named style is shown and designable; one no factory can make yet is offered as a quotation request, never as an instant order — and the request is the demand signal that tells the Partner team which capability to add next.
